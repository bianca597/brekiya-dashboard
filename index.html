competitive keyword insight
what competitor keyword data tells us about their strategy
nurtec strategy signal
Heavy branded defense — bidding on own brand at pos. 1 across 90K+ monthly searches. Rimegepant ($14 CPC) signals INN conquest targeting. "Generic" bidding ($15–24 CPC) captures cost-conscious patients actively seeking alternatives.
ubrelvy strategy signal
Access-first keyword strategy — top traffic from "side effects," "copay card," and "coupon" terms. Lower overall search volume than Nurtec signals smaller active patient base. Savings messaging dominates their creative approach.

identified opportunities for brekiya
🎯 access + savings messaging
Ubrelvy drives significant volume from "copay card" and "coupon" searches ($9.20 CPC). Brekiya has no savings-focused ad copy visible in market. Patients actively searching for cost help are going to competitors first.
opportunity: add savings-focused ad variations targeting cost + access queries
🎯 "how it works" / mechanism
Both Nurtec and Ubrelvy run "how does it work" and "see how this treatment works" copy across top-traffic terms. This educational angle builds trust at the awareness stage and Brekiya is currently absent from it.
opportunity: develop mechanism-focused creative and test on mid-funnel query types
🎯 competitor conquest opportunity
Neither Nurtec nor Ubrelvy appears to be running conquest campaigns against each other. Brekiya could own branded competitor keywords ("nurtec alternative," "ubrelvy alternative") at potentially lower CPCs with differentiation copy.
opportunity: test competitor-branded conquest terms with comparison landing pages
🎯 brekiya's current edge: symptoms
Brekiya's symptom/condition campaign targets unbranded migraine queries that neither competitor dominates. This is a whitespace Brekiya already occupies — increasing budget here captures patients earlier in the decision journey.
action: protect and scale symptoms investment — this is Brekiya's clearest competitive moat
